Donor Pathway

I Want to Donate — The Pledging Pathway

How a voluntary pledge works and the difference between deceased and living organ donation.

Deceased Organ Donation

Occurs when an individual has been medically certified as brain stem dead in an ICU. Next of kin confirm the consent at the hospital before retrieval by authorized government teams.

Tissues such as corneas (eyes) can also be donated following cardiac death at home or at the hospital.

Living Organ Donation

A healthy living individual may donate one kidney or a portion of liver to an immediate family member under strict statutory authorization committee approval and clinical testing.

Living donation is governed strictly to protect donor health and prohibit any commercial transactions.

Official Government of India Registry

National Organ & Tissue Transplant Organisation (NOTTO)

Register your voluntary donor pledge directly on the authorized Government of India NOTTO registry (MoHFW) or via your Ayushman Bharat Health Account (ABHA).

Recipient Guidance

I Need a Transplant — The Patient Journey

Understanding how patients with chronic organ failure navigate toward authorized transplant centers.

1

Clinical Evaluation

Patient undergoes thorough clinical workup by a specialized medical board.

2

Authorized Centre Referral

Patient is referred to a state-licensed Government or Empaneled Transplant Hospital.

3

Official Registry Listing

The authorized hospital registers the patient on the official national/state waiting list.

4

Transparent Allocation

Organ allocation is strictly automated and governed by authorized medical authorities.

5

Authorized Transplant

Surgeons at the certified hospital perform the transplant procedure under documented oversight.

Family Conversation Guide

Talk to Your Family About Your Wishes

In India, next-of-kin confirmation is required at the hospital during deceased donation. Communicating with your family ensures your wishes are known and respected.

1. Share Your Reasons

Explain why you wish to donate organs or corneas to help someone with organ failure or blindness.

2. Answer Common Concerns

Reassure family members that donation is handled with surgical respect and does not interfere with funeral customs.

3. Provide Official Information

Share official national resources and clarify that no financial transactions are ever involved.

Educational Tool Notice: This conversation guide is for family awareness only. It is not a legal consent document or substitute for statutory hospital protocols.

Myths vs. Facts

Evidence-aligned clarifications on common organ donation questions.

Myth: Doctors will not try to save my life if they know I pledged to donate organs.
Fact: The emergency ICU team treating you is completely separate from transplant coordination teams. Saving your life is always the first and only clinical priority.
Myth: Organ donation disfigures the body for funeral ceremonies.
Fact: Surgical retrieval is conducted with extreme clinical respect and care, similar to standard surgery, allowing customary open-casket or traditional funeral rites.
Myth: Organs can be bought or sold through private websites or platforms.
Fact: Buying or selling human organs is strictly illegal under the Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act. All donation is non-commercial, voluntary, and ethically governed.
Myth: Living organ donation and deceased body donation are the same thing.
Fact: Organ donation involves specific viable organs/tissues for patient transplantation, whereas whole-body anatomical donation is given to medical colleges for scientific education.
